## 1. Exact product specification

**Sell this exact spec. Reject suppliers who miss any line.**

| Spec | Requirement | Why it's non-negotiable |
|---|---|---|
| **Suction** | **≥12,000Pa** | Below 10,000Pa fails on double-coated breeds — the #1 refund driver |
| **Noise** | **≤55dB** | ≥60dB stresses pets. This is the entire brand position |
| **Tools** | 5-in-1 minimum | Deshedding brush, clipper, slicker brush, nozzle, crevice tool |

| **Dustbin** | ≥2L (3L preferred) | Sub-2L means emptying mid-groom on a large dog |
| **Modes** | 3 suction levels | Low mode = acclimation ramp for nervous dogs |
| **Cert** | **CE, FCC, RoHS** | Required for US import; also a trust badge on the PDP |
| **Power** | Corded | **Deliberate.** Avoids lithium air-freight restrictions in Q4 |

> **On corded vs cordless:** choose corded. It removes the lithium-battery shipping class that
> would otherwise wreck your Q4 delivery times, and for a stationary at-home grooming session
> there is no real use-case loss. This is a constraint turned into an advantage.

### 2.1 Personal Attributes — the rules that keep you live

Meta's most-triggered rejection. The test: **does the copy imply you know something about the
viewer?** Talk about the *product* and the *dog*, never about *you the reader*.

| ❌ Never write | ✅ Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Is your dog scared of grooming?" | "Most grooming vacuums hit 65dB. Dogs hear that as a threat." |
| "Struggling with pet hair everywhere?" | "12,000Pa pulls loose undercoat before it reaches the couch." |
| "You're wasting $100 a month on groomers" | "The national average dog groom is $100 a visit." |
| "Your anxious dog needs this" | "Rated ≤55dB — quiet enough for nervous dogs." |
| "Tired of vacuuming every day?" | "Collects the fur at the source." |

**Rule of thumb: state facts about the world, not assumptions about the reader.**
Second-person questions about the viewer's situation are the trigger. Declaratives about
the product and the category are safe.

### 2.2 Claims you may make (substantiated)

- ✅ "≥12,000Pa suction" — spec-backed, keep supplier documentation
- ✅ "≤55dB" — spec-backed
- ✅ "The average US dog groom costs $100 a visit" — published industry data, cite it
- ✅ "Pays for itself in under two grooming visits" — arithmetic from a cited figure
- ✅ "5 professional tools in one kit" — factual

### 2.3 Claims to avoid

- ❌ Anything implying medical/therapeutic benefit to the animal ("cures anxiety," "treats
  skin conditions") — pet health claims require veterinary substantiation
- ❌ "Vet recommended" / "vet approved" without a real, documented veterinarian endorsement
- ❌ "#1 rated" / "best grooming vacuum" without a citable ranking source
- ❌ Fake countdown timers or fabricated stock counts — misleading-claims violation *and* they
  now cross-check your landing page

### 2.4 Landing-page parity (new in 2026)

Ad copy, landing page, and image metadata are reviewed **as a single compliance unit**. A clean
ad pointed at a non-compliant page is rejected at the ad level. Therefore:

- The dB and Pa figures in the ad **must** appear on the product page
- No health claims anywhere on site, including FAQ, reviews you import, and blog content
- **Filter imported reviews** — an imported review saying "cured my dog's anxiety" is your
  compliance problem now
- Strip health-claim keywords from image alt text and filenames

## 6. Target audience

### 6.1 Primary — "The Shedding-Season Dog Parent"

- **Age 35–65** (core Meta demo; Gen X dog ownership **+12% YoY**)
- **Gender:** ~65% female skew, but **do not exclude men** — test broad
- **Geo:** US first. Then CA, UK, AU
- **Household:** owns a **double-coated or heavy-shedding breed** — Golden Retriever, German
  Shepherd, Husky, Labrador, Australian Shepherd, Corgi, Samoyed, Bernese Mountain Dog
- **Income:** $50k–$120k — spends on the dog regardless
- **Psychographic:** dog is family, not property. Posts dog photos. Has a lint roller in the car.

### 6.2 Secondary

- Long-haired **cat** owners (Maine Coon, Persian, Ragdoll)
- **Q4 gift buyers** — spouses and adult children of dog owners. Different creative entirely
  (see gift angle in `05`)
- **Multi-pet households** — highest AOV, best bundle target

### 6.3 Pain points, ranked by ad-hook strength

1. **The dog is genuinely frightened of grooming** — highest emotional charge, and the one
   competitors can't answer
2. **Groomer cost** — $40–$100/visit; **$100 national average**; $75–$125+ for small-dog full
   service; $200+ for XL dogs. That's ~$600–$1,200/year
3. **Fur on everything** — couch, car, clothes, food
4. **Booking friction** — 2–3 week waits, transport stress
5. **DIY makes a worse mess** — loose fur airborne across the house
6. **Household allergies**

## 7. Facebook ad angles & hooks

### Angle 1 — **The Noise Angle** ⭐ *lead with this*
*Position: it's not the grooming he hates, it's the sound.*
- "Most grooming vacuums run at 65 decibels. Dogs hear that as a threat."
- "There's a reason he hides when the clippers come out."
- "55 decibels. That's quieter than a dishwasher."

### Angle 2 — **The $100 Math**
*Position: rational justification for an emotional purchase.*
- "The national average dog groom is $100 a visit."
- "Two grooming appointments. That's the whole cost of this."
- "$1,200 a year, or once."

### Angle 3 — **The Satisfying Demo** (best cold-traffic hook)
*Position: no words needed — just the fur lifting off the coat.*
- Open on the undercoat visibly leaving the dog and vanishing into the hose. No VO for 3s.
- "This is 12,000 pascals in real time."

### Angle 4 — **The Couch** (compliant before/after)
- "Same couch. Ten minutes apart."
- "This is one week of golden retriever."

### Angle 5 — **Breed-Specific** (highest CTR, cheapest CPC)
- "Golden retriever owners — this one's for you."
- "If you own a husky, you already know what September means."
- Build one creative per breed. Breed-specific creative consistently beats generic pet creative.

### Angle 6 — **The Gift** (switch on 1 Nov)
- "The gift every dog owner actually wants."
- "For the person whose entire wardrobe is covered in fur."
- Targets the *buyer*, not the user. Different audience, different creative.

### Angle 7 — **Spec Comparison**
- "Quiet OR powerful. Every other kit makes you choose."
- Side-by-side: 65dB/8,000Pa vs 55dB/12,000Pa.

### Hook bank — first 3 seconds

1. "Your dog isn't dramatic. He's just hearing 65 decibels."
2. "This is what $100 a month at the groomer actually buys you."
3. *[silent shot of fur lifting off coat]* → "Wait for it."
4. "I stopped booking the groomer in March. Here's what happened."
5. "Same couch. Ten minutes apart."
6. "Golden retriever owners, I'm so sorry. And you're welcome."
7. "12,000 pascals. 55 decibels. Everyone else picks one."
8. "He used to hide under the bed when I got the clippers out."
9. "Three weeks to get a grooming appointment. Or ten minutes."
10. "This is one week of shedding. One week."

**Winner formula for cold traffic:** Angle 3 hook (silent demo) → Angle 1 (noise) → Angle 2
($100 math) → CTA. Full script in `04-higgsfield-prompts.md`.
